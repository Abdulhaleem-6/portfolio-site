# PRODUCT.md

## Product purpose

The personal portfolio of Abdulhaleem Sanuth, an AI-infrastructure and distributed-systems
engineer in Lagos. It exists to help technical recruiters, product managers, and hiring managers
understand within ten seconds that he solves production problems with practical engineering.
Four case studies carry the argument. Everything else is scaffolding.

## Register

brand — the design is the product.

## Users

Technical recruiters, product managers, and hiring managers looking for a grounded engineer who
can explain the problem, the trade-off, and the result. They are skimming and may open exactly one
case study before deciding whether to start a conversation.

Secondary: senior engineers who want evidence behind the high-level story.

## Physical scene

A staff engineer opens the link at 11pm on a 27-inch display in a dark room, one tab among
thirty, deciding whether to keep reading. That scene forces a dark primary theme, high contrast,
and information density over decoration.

## Brand voice

Three physical-object words: **machined, measured, unsentimental.**

The reference objects are an engineering drawing's title block, a calibration certificate, and a
Swiss railway timetable. Not a magazine, not a startup landing page. The site should read as
instrumentation: everything labelled, everything sourced, nothing decorative.

The written voice is already set by the content and must not change: plain, specific, willing to
say what went wrong. "Every number carries its basis, or it comes off."

## Strategic principles

1. **Evidence over assertion.** Five of the seven codebases behind this site are private, so every
   case pairs plain-language claims with measured figures and a clear explanation of their source.
2. **Density is respect.** The audience reads fast. Whitespace that carries no information is
   wasted; a data table beats a card.
3. **The typography is the art direction.** No photography, no illustration. Giant numerals,
   business callouts, and simplified system diagrams carry the visual story.
4. **Admit the flaws.** Every case study ends with what the author would change. This is the
   single most persuasive section on the site and must never be softened.

## Anti-references

- Soft SaaS template: rounded cards, drop shadows, pastel gradients, pill tags.
- Any page whose fonts are Inter, Plus Jakarta Sans, Space Grotesk, or DM Sans.
- Terminal-cliche developer aesthetics: neon green on black, fake CRT scanlines, blinking cursors.
- Editorial-magazine drift: display serif with italic drop caps and broadsheet columns.
- The hero-metric template: a big number over a small label over supporting stats.
